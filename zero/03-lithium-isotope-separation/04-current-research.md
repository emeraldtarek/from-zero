# 04 — Current Research Frontier (2020–2026)

## Prerequisite check

You've absorbed `01-` (why), `02-` (physics), and `03-` (the methods).
You know what α is, what a cascade is, and you can name a dozen
different ways humans have tried (or are trying) to separate Li-6 from
Li-7.

This file zooms into the **active research literature** of the past few
years and tells you who is publishing, what they're claiming, and which
directions are gaining momentum vs. blocked.

A general note: **most of the credible work is in the 2022–2026 window**
because the fusion-industry urgency has reactivated a field that was
relatively dormant from the 1980s through the 2010s. If you're reading
papers, default to "the more recent the better" unless looking
explicitly at COLEX-era reference material.

## Recommended reading order (the 8 papers to start with)

If you have time for nothing else, read these in order:

1. **Symons (1985), *Separation Science and Technology***. The classic
   review of COLEX-era and immediately post-COLEX chemistry. Establishes
   the framework, the α benchmarks, the equilibrium-vs-kinetic
   distinction. Background on the historical industrial process.
2. **Stephan, Asakura et al. (2008), *Solvent Extraction and Ion
   Exchange*** (or other 2000s-era Symons-citation reviews). The
   transition from COLEX-era thinking to the modern crown-ether and
   ion-exchange literature.
3. **Cui, Zhao, Wang et al. (2021), *Chemical Communications* /
   adjacent**. The ion-pair LLX breakthrough — FeCl₃ + crown ethers
   yielding $D_\text{Li}$ = 54 with α ≈ 1.04. Reframes the practical
   feasibility of crown-ether LLX.
4. **Wild, Jin, Yang (2022), *Journal of the Electrochemical
   Society***. The foundational finite-element model of electrochemical
   Li-isotope deposition. Establishes the ESIX framework. This is the
   Yuan Yang group at Columbia — likely the closest US-Ivy group to
   the user's friend's cohort.
5. **Badea, Chiriac, Stoian et al. (2023), *Materials* (PMC10222844)**.
   Comprehensive 2020s-era review of all Li-isotope-separation
   methods. The single best post-COLEX-era literature review;
   covers crown ethers, ionic liquids, MOFs, electrodialysis, ELEX.
6. **Carrillo, ..., Banerjee (2025), *Chem***. The ζ-V₂O₅
   electrochemical-intercalation paper from the Texas A&M Banerjee
   group. The first non-mercury demonstration approaching COLEX
   single-cycle enrichment. Field's most-discussed recent result.
7. **Battistella, Aksu, Persson, ... (2026), *ACS Energy Letters***.
   Depth-resolved Li-6 fractionation in operating battery electrodes.
   Methodologically novel; bridges battery science and isotope work.
8. **Ward, Sandri, ... (May 2026), *arXiv:2605.04707***. The mercury-
   scalability paper. A preprint, but its data on fusion-scale
   Li-6 demand vs. mercury supply is sobering and influential.

After these, branch into specific method-family deep dives.

## A. Active US research programs

### Columbia University — Yuan Yang group (SEAS)
- **Most active US Ivy-League group** publishing on Li-6/Li-7
  separation in 2022–2026.
- Foundational paper: Wild, Jin, Yang (2022) on finite-element
  modeling of electrochemical Li-isotope deposition.
- Subsequent work on intercalation electrodes, kinetic vs equilibrium
  isotope effects in battery materials.
- Group focus: bridging electrochemical engineering and isotope
  separation; computational modeling supporting experimental work.

If the user's friend works on electrochemical methods — which is
plausible given how active that direction is right now — the Yang
group is the most likely Ivy-League home. (We do not know for certain.)

### Texas A&M — Sarbajit Banerjee group
- Not Ivy League, but **the most active US academic group** on Li-6/
  Li-7 separation in 2024–2026.
- Landmark 2025 paper: Carrillo et al. in *Chem*, applying ζ-V₂O₅
  electrochemical intercalation to Li-isotope enrichment.
- Group focus: tunnel-structure intercalation hosts (V₂O₅ polymorphs,
  niobate-based), battery-electrode chemistry repurposed for
  isotope work.

### Marathon Fusion (San Francisco)
- **Most active US commercial program** on Li-6 enrichment for fusion.
- ARPA-E OPEN 2024 grant: $3.63M, active March 2026.
- Approach: plasma centrifuge.
- No published performance data as of mid-2026.

### National laboratories
- **ORNL**: continues research on Li-isotope separation, including
  electrochemical methods, ion-exchange, and material development.
  Holds historical COLEX expertise + ongoing isotope-production
  facilities (calutron-class instruments).
- **LLNL**: laser separation legacy and continued AVLIS-related work.
  Less open publication recently.
- **Sandia**: materials and characterization support.

### Hexium / ASP Isotopes (Texas / California)
- US-based AVLIS commercialization startup.
- Pursuing Li-6 (and other isotopes) at commercial scale.
- Public information sparse; performance data confidential.

### Other US academic groups
- Stanford, MIT, Berkeley have *adjacent* work in battery materials,
  membrane science, MOFs — but as of 2026 published literature, they
  are not specifically chasing Li isotope separation as their primary
  research target. (Adjacent expertise is convertible — they are
  one paper away from being in the field if motivated.)

## B. International research programs

### Cambridge University — Nitschke group
- Not traditionally a separations group; built coordination cages.
- Two 2025 papers in *Chem*: switchable Li extraction by coordination
  cages, one proton-driven, one light-driven.
- These are proof-of-concept demonstrations; the cages bind Li⁺ with
  high selectivity vs. Na/K, and the *switch* allows release on
  demand (eliminating the regeneration-step problem of LLX).
- Most intellectually novel direction in the field; isotope α not yet
  measured but plausibly in the 1.02–1.05 range.

### KIT (Karlsruhe Institute of Technology) — Giegerich group
- Lead European authority on TRANSAT (Trans-disciplinary Approach to
  Tritium Self-sufficiency).
- Published the canonical fusion-blanket Li-6 demand calculations
  (Giegerich et al., *Fusion Engineering and Design*, 2019).
- Strongly connected to EU-DEMO planning.

### EPFL — Marliere, Hu, Buonsanti groups
- Active work on Li/Mg separation membranes, MOF-based selectivity.
- Bridge to isotope-relevant chemistry.

### Imperial College London
- Membrane chemistry and ionic-liquid extraction, somewhat focused on
  lithium recovery from brines but feeding into isotope-separation
  options.

### China — Shanghai Institute of Applied Physics (SINAP); Tsinghua;
### Lanzhou Institute of Chemical Physics; CAS Institute of Nuclear
### Energy Safety Technology
- China's MSR program (TMSR-LF1 reactor in Wuwei) is driving
  significant Li-7 demand and aggressive R&D into Li-isotope
  separation.
- Many publications in Chinese journals; some accessible internationally
  via *Separation and Purification Technology*, *Journal of Membrane
  Science*, etc.
- Likely the most well-funded Li-isotope-separation R&D ecosystem
  worldwide as of 2026, though publishing tends to be domestic-leading.

### Japan — Tohoku, Kyoto, Tokyo Institute of Technology
- Long history in nuclear chemistry and crown-ether extraction.
- Kyoto Fusioneering (commercial spinoff) pursuing fusion-blanket
  systems including Li-6 enrichment supply chain.

### Korea — KAIST, KAERI
- Active in ion-exchange chromatography and crown-ether systems.

### Russia — TVEL / NCCP Novosibirsk
- The dominant **commercial** producer of enriched Li-7 (PWR-grade)
  worldwide; supplies up to 80% of world Li-7 demand.
- Reportedly modernized and doubled Li-7 capacity in 2013.
- Publications largely in Russian-language journals; methods used
  remain partially opaque (mercury-electrolysis variant of COLEX is
  the public attribution).
- The geopolitical dimension is significant: Russian Li-7 supply is
  the sole non-Cold-War-stockpile source for much of the world.

### UK — UKAEA, Bangor University
- UKAEA Fusion Materials Programme funding (~£18M+ across multiple
  projects 2023–2026) targets Li-6 separation explicitly.
- Bangor University grant (announced 2023): microbe-mediated
  Li-6 fractionation (biological/biomimetic approach).

## C. The current research themes — and what to read for each

### Theme 1 — Replacing mercury with non-toxic chemistry

The post-COLEX problem. Crown ethers, calixarenes, MOFs, ionic
liquids, polymer-inclusion membranes. Most of the academic
literature.

- **Cui 2021 (ion-pair B12C4/B15C5/FeCl₃)** is the keystone paper.
- **Badea 2023** is the comprehensive review.
- **MOF / COF frameworks** with crown-ether functionalization are
  hot topic in 2023–2024.

### Theme 2 — Electrochemical / battery-material approaches

The convergence of battery science and isotope separation. Faradaic
intercalation hosts (V₂O₅, MnO₂ polymorphs, layered titanates) under
applied potential.

- **Wild, Jin, Yang 2022** for the modeling foundation.
- **Carrillo, Banerjee 2025** for the experimental landmark.
- **Battistella 2026** for cross-disciplinary methodology (battery
  characterization techniques used to measure isotope effects in
  electrodes).

### Theme 3 — Membrane / continuous-flow processing

Replacing batch LLX with continuous membranes (PIM, SLM, MOF films,
NF, monovalent-selective ED).

- **Selemion CSO and Neosepta CIMS** (commercial monovalent-selective
  CEMs) appear in element-level Li/Mg work and might bridge to
  isotope work.
- **MOF/COF membrane** literature 2023–2024 reports α up to 1.053.
- Stability under real-feed conditions is the open problem.

### Theme 4 — Plasma and laser approaches

The "physical" methods that bypass chemistry entirely.

- **Marathon Fusion** (plasma centrifuge): proprietary; no public data.
- **Hexium / ASP Isotopes** (AVLIS): proprietary; no public data.
- Open-literature laser physics on Li transitions: well-established;
  applications papers behind commercial firewalls.

### Theme 5 — Computational ligand and material design

Using DFT + path-integral methods (PIMD) to *predict* isotope
selectivity in proposed materials before synthesizing them. Tools
exist (CP2K, i-PI, ASE) but no group has yet systematically applied
them to material design for Li-isotope separation. **This is one of
the most under-explored opportunities in the field**, given how cheap
computation has become relative to experimental synthesis.

### Theme 6 — Coordination cages and switchable extraction

The Nitschke-group direction. Coordination cages with on-demand
release (proton-, light-, or redox-triggered). Could solve the
regeneration-loop problem of LLX.

- **Nitschke 2025 (two papers in *Chem*)**.
- Isotope α not yet measured but plausibly in the 1.02–1.05 range
  given the crown-ether-like binding chemistry.

### Theme 7 — Biological / biomimetic

The Bangor / UKAEA microbial fractionation work. Long shot but
funded and active.

## D. Open research questions

What the field hasn't answered yet, ordered roughly by impact:

1. **Can any non-mercury chemistry achieve COLEX α (~1.05) at
   industrial throughput?** Cui 2021's $D_\text{Li}$ = 54 with α =
   1.04 is the closest answer to date, but it's bench-scale.
2. **Can an electrochemical intercalation process (ESIX) be run
   continuously at industrial scale with COLEX-class α?** Carrillo
   2025 suggests yes in principle; engineering scale-up is the next
   step.
3. **Is the plasma-centrifuge approach industrially feasible?**
   Marathon Fusion's results will be a watershed moment if disclosed.
4. **What is the energy cost (kWh / kg of enriched Li-6) for each
   non-mercury method?** Strikingly, this is rarely reported in the
   literature — every paper tends to focus on α and mostly ignore
   energy or cost. This is one of the biggest data gaps.
5. **MOF/COF stability**: can a Li-isotope-selective MOF be made
   stable enough for industrial cycling?
6. **Computational design**: can PIMD-based screening predict α for
   proposed materials accurately enough to guide synthesis?
7. **Cascading and coupling**: which combination of methods (e.g.,
   ESIX as primary + AVLIS as polishing) gives the most cost-
   effective full enrichment from 7.59% to >90%? No published
   integrated process design.

## E. Reading the literature — a practical guide

### Where to look
- **Web of Science / Scopus**: search "lithium isotope separation"
  filtered to 2020+.
- **arXiv** physics.chem-ph and cond-mat sections for preprints.
- **Specific journals to follow**: *Separation and Purification
  Technology*, *Journal of Membrane Science*, *Industrial &
  Engineering Chemistry Research*, *Separation Science and Technology*,
  *Fusion Engineering and Design*, *Nuclear Engineering and Design*,
  *Journal of Electrochemical Society*, *Chem* (Cell Press), *ACS
  Energy Letters*, *Nature Communications* / *Communications
  Chemistry*.
- **Conference proceedings**: Symposium on Fusion Engineering (SOFE),
  International Symposium on Fusion Nuclear Technology (ISFNT),
  ANS Winter Meeting; for the chemistry side, Gordon Research
  Conferences on Separations.

### What to look for in a paper
1. **Type of α reported**: single-stage vs. cascade; equilibrium vs.
   kinetic.
2. **System composition**: what's the feed (LiCl in water? Li metal?
   LiOH? brine?), what's the contacting phase (organic with which
   crown? polymer-inclusion membrane?), at what concentration?
3. **Scale**: is this glassware (mL), bench (L), pilot (m³)?
4. **Throughput**: rate (mol Li / s), or just contact-time
   demonstrations.
5. **Energy data**: rare but valuable when present (kWh/kg).
6. **Stability data**: how long does the system run before
   degradation?
7. **Comparison to COLEX**: most papers cite COLEX α as the
   benchmark; how do they compare?

### Red flags
- Papers reporting impressive lab-α with no scaling discussion.
  Many anthraquinone-crown-ether studies report α > 1.5 in a single
  flask but have never been operated as a continuous cascade.
- "Compared to COLEX, our method is X times better" claims
  *without* matching the SWU (separative work) framework.
- Papers from non-fusion-relevant feeds (e.g., extracts from
  spent battery leachate) generalizing to fusion-grade
  applicability without cross-checks.
- Single-publication "miraculous" α values not replicated
  independently.

## Common misconceptions

- **"The literature has converged on a winning method."** It has
  not. Multiple methods are at the same maturity (bench/lab) with
  similar α (~1.04–1.05). Nobody has demonstrated industrial-scale
  non-mercury enrichment yet.
- **"More papers = more progress."** The volume of publications has
  exploded since 2020, but most of them iterate on minor variants
  (different crown ether, different solvent). The major
  conceptual advances are roughly: (1) Cui 2021 ion-pair, (2)
  Carrillo/Banerjee 2025 ζ-V₂O₅, (3) Nitschke 2025 coordination
  cages.
- **"The Russians have it figured out."** Russia has industrial
  COLEX-derived production; whether they have made non-mercury
  innovations is opaque. Their published literature is sparse
  internationally.
- **"Computational design will solve this any year now."** Tools
  exist; uptake has been slow, possibly because the experimental
  community and the computational community don't fully overlap.
  This is a real opportunity.

## Self-check questions

1. The Carrillo/Banerjee 2025 paper achieved COLEX-comparable
   single-cycle enrichment using ζ-V₂O₅. What's the underlying
   mechanism, and why is this conceptually important even if industrial
   scale-up is unproven?
2. The Nitschke group's coordination cages allow *switchable*
   extraction (release triggered by light or pH). Why is this
   architecturally different from conventional crown-ether LLX, and
   what regeneration-loop problem does it potentially solve?

   in this field, given that the experimental community is well-funded?
