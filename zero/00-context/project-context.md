# Project Context

The original brief, captured verbatim in spirit, with structure added.

## Origin

Tarek's friend is doing post-doctoral research at an Ivy League US university.
Her research is focused on **separating lithium-6 (Li-6) and lithium-7 (Li-7) ions**.

Tarek currently understands "absolutely nothing" about this domain — neither the
chemistry side nor the water-treatment / process-engineering side. He wants to
fix that, fast and rigorously.

## What we are doing

We are running a **two-track project**:

### Track 1 — The lithium learning journey
Educate Tarek systematically from fundamentals so that he can:
- Comprehend the **chemistry** of atoms, isotopes, and ions
- Comprehend the **domain space** of water treatment
- Comprehend the **problem space** of Li-6/Li-7 separation
- Read and engage with associated literature
- Participate substantively in communications around the subject
- Eventually **collaborate on ideating and proposing separation strategies**

### Track 2 — Synthesizing a reusable learning skill
Track our progress and approach throughout, then perform a **post-mortem** that
distills the structure and patterns into a **reusable Claude Code skill** for
future "from-zero" learning journeys in any domain.

## Why

- **Domain coherence**: Tarek's friend is a domain expert; he wants to be conversant.
- **Strategic relevance**: Li-6 isotope separation is foundational to nuclear
  fusion (tritium breeding), to certain fission technologies, and to several
  niche scientific applications. The supply chain for enriched Li-6 is a known
  geopolitical bottleneck.
- **Intellectual interest**: separating two isotopes that differ by ~17% in
  mass and almost nothing in chemistry is a beautifully constrained problem.
- **Reusability**: a battle-tested "learn a domain from zero" framework is
  generally useful far beyond lithium.

## How (the method)

1. **Fundamentals first.** Build chemistry from the absolute building blocks
   (matter → atoms → subatomic particles → charge → elements → periodic table
   → atomic mass → isotopes → ions → bonding → solutions → lithium specifics).
   Skip nothing. The user wants the floor solid before he stands on it.
2. **Layer in water treatment.** Once chemistry is solid, learn how water
   treatment works as a domain — methods, processes, ion-handling techniques.
   This is the practical "process engineering" container that surrounds the
   isotope-separation problem.
3. **Drill into the lithium problem.** With foundation and domain in place,
   go deep on Li-6/Li-7 separation specifically — why it's hard, why it
   matters, and every method humanity has invented for it (COLEX, electro-
   magnetic, laser, electrochemical, membrane, MOF, crown-ether, etc.).
4. **Ideate together.** With Tarek genuinely up to speed, collaborate on
   proposing separation approaches. (This phase is open-ended; success looks
   like a written proposal that his friend would consider non-trivial.)
5. **Synthesize the meta-skill.** Convert the journey's structure, patterns,
   and pedagogical moves into a reusable Claude Code skill.

## Constraints / working style (from the user)

- **No chemistry background, but not lacking capacity.** Failed Chem 101 in
  undergrad three times due to inattention; was top of his high-school
  chemistry class. He learns extremely quickly when engaged.
- **Doctorate-level depth.** Pop-science explanations will not satisfy.
- **Mental model: build from blocks.** "Grok any subject" by starting from the
  atomic-level building blocks and stacking up.
- **Persistence to disk is non-negotiable.** Everything goes into `.md` files
  so any session can be picked up fresh.
- **Track knowledge growth explicitly** in a knowledge tracker file.
- **Track project progress explicitly** so the post-mortem has raw material.
- **Bias to action.** Scaffold first, research in parallel, deep-write, then
  teach.

## Definition of done

- **Track 1 done** when Tarek can read a current Li-6/Li-7 separation paper end
  to end and either summarize it accurately or articulate the specific gaps in
  his understanding — and when he has co-authored at least one written
  separation-strategy proposal with Claude.
- **Track 2 done** when the reusable skill exists in a form that another user
  could invoke and follow for a different domain (e.g., "learn molecular
  biology from zero").
